7 restaurant website mistakes that cost you reservations

The 7 mistakes we see most often on restaurant websites — and how to fix them one by one.

📅 November 5, 2026 ⏱ 8 min read By Soul Webs

A badly built website quietly sends guests to the restaurant down the street. People open the site, can't find what they need in a few seconds, close the tab and book somewhere else. These are the 7 mistakes we see most often on restaurant websites.

Mistake 1: A PDF menu

Uploading your menu as a PDF is mistake number one. Guests open it on their phone, pinch to zoom, scroll around a tiny screen and give up before dessert.

Why it fails

  • Google doesn't index PDF content well. You miss searches like "birria tacos in Houston".
  • PDFs aren't responsive: on a phone they're tiny.
  • Change a price and forget to re-upload, and the menu is wrong for months.

The fix

A real HTML menu, split into sections (starters, mains, desserts, drinks), with prices you can update instantly and a Spanish version if your guests need it. The quickest route: connect OpenTable or Resy or a custom system.

Mistake 2: No visible "Reserve" button at the top

If someone opens your website, it's because they're thinking about booking. Don't make them hunt for the button. It should be:

  • In the hero (the first screen)
  • Sticky in the menu as they scroll
  • In the footer

What a sticky mobile reservation button usually does: more bookings.

Mistake 3: A slow website (over 3 seconds to load)

Google's own research shows that the chance of a visitor leaving rises sharply as load time goes past a few seconds.

Most common causes

  • Food or dining-room photos weighing 5–8 MB each
  • WordPress with 15 active plugins
  • Cheap shared hosting
  • Autoplay background video (kill it)

How to measure it

Go to PageSpeed Insights from Google: paste your URL and check the result. Under 80 on mobile is a problem. Under 50 is urgent.

Mistake 4: No online reservations

Today, many guests expect to book without calling: on your website, in an app, or through OpenTable or Resy. If the phone is the only way to book, you lose everyone who'd rather not call.

Mistake 5: No visible map or hours

It's surprising how many restaurant websites don't show the address and hours on the homepage. They hide them on a "Contact" page three clicks deep.

What should always be visible

  • Address with Embedded Google Maps (a map, not just text)
  • Full hours including closures, holidays and "kitchen open until X"
  • Phone number tap-to-call (on a phone it should start the call right away)
  • Text or WhatsApp if you use it
  • Visible email

Mistake 6: Food photos that look like they're from 2010

Photos sell the food. Harsh flash, flat white backgrounds and messy plating say "we don't care how it looks" — and what does that say about the actual dish?

The minimum

  • Light natural, from the side, not direct flash from above.
  • Same framing and style in every photo (visual consistency).
  • At least 8–12 good photos. Few but great, not 50 average ones.
  • Optimized: each photo under 200 KB, in WebP format.

Mistake 7: No reviews or social proof

Before booking, new guests look for reassurance: has anyone else been here and loved it? If your website doesn't show:

  • Google or Yelp reviews
  • Logos of press that mentioned you
  • Awards or industry recognition

...they close the tab and book where they do see them.

How to tell if your website has these problems

Run this quick test on your own website:

  1. Open your website on your phone (not your computer).
  2. Time yourself: how many seconds to find the address?
  3. How many seconds to make a reservation?
  4. Does the menu load in under 3 seconds?
  5. Do the food photos make you want to go?
  6. Do you see reviews or any social proof?

If any of these takes more than 10 seconds or goes wrong, you have a problem that's costing you reservations every day.

The two highest-impact fixes

If you can only fix two things today, make it these:

  1. A visible, sticky "Reserve" button on mobile that opens OpenTable, Resy or your booking page directly.
  2. A real digital menu (not a PDF) with great photos of your 8–10 signature dishes.

Those two changes alone remove the biggest reasons guests give up on a restaurant website.

FAQ

Is my own website worth it if I'm already on OpenTable?

Usually, yes. OpenTable is great for discovery, but regulars search for you by name on Google. And with your own site and booking widget, you control the first impression.

What's the best format for a digital menu?

Plain HTML, split into sections (starters, mains, desserts, drinks), with visible prices, a Spanish version if you need it, and allergen info. Fast to load and quick to update.

OpenTable or Resy?

Both are widely used in the US. OpenTable has the biggest diner network; Resy is popular with chef-driven and trendier spots. Compare current pricing for your volume — we can connect either one to your website.

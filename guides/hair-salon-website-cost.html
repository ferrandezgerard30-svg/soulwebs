How much a hair salon website costs: real prices in 2026

DIY builders, freelancers, agencies or pay-only-if-you-love-it: real 2026 US price ranges and what each option includes, so there are no surprises.

📅 November 5, 2026 ⏱ 7 min read By Soul Webs

In the US, a hair salon website can cost anywhere from $0 to $10,000+ in 2026, depending on who builds it and how. Most small salons land somewhere between a DIY builder and a freelance project.

Before comparing prices, there's a more important decision: what your salon needs from its website. "Being on Google" is different from "getting online bookings" or "competing with the chain down the street". The right price depends on that.

Here are the four main ways salons buy a website in the US in 2026, what each includes, and which makes sense for where your business is right now.

Option 1: DIY builders (~$200–$600/year)

Platforms like Wix, Squarespace, Webflow or good old WordPress.com let you build a site yourself from ready-made templates. The cost is low: roughly $17–$50 a month depending on the plan (2026 list prices), plus your domain.

The good: low price, no tech person needed, live from day one.

The bad: it looks like a template, your local competition may use the same one, the SEO is generic and speed is often mediocre. Plenty of DIY sites get abandoned after a few months.

"It's like wearing a chain-store uniform when your shop has its own personality. It works, but it doesn't stand out."

Option 2: Freelancer (~$800–$4,000)

Freelancers typically charge somewhere around $800–$4,000 for a small business website in 2026, often more in big US cities. That range covers everyone from design students to established freelancers.

The good: custom design, you talk directly to the person building it, lots of flexibility.

The bad: the result depends hugely on who you hire. Ask for a portfolio and references — and even then it can go either way. Ongoing maintenance is often left unclear.

What a mid-range project should include

  • Custom design (not a template)
  • Hosting for at least the first year
  • Domain management
  • Technical SEO basics (Schema.org, sitemap, robots.txt)
  • Booking integration (Booksy, Fresha, Vagaro or Square)
  • Mobile-friendly, no exceptions
  • At least 6 months of support after launch

If they offer less than that, be careful. If they promise "unlimited forever", also be careful.

Option 3: Agencies (~$2,000–$10,000+)

An agency with a team — designer, copywriter, developer, account manager — usually starts at a few thousand dollars for a small salon and can go well past $10,000 for chains or premium salons with several locations.

The good: much more capacity: full branding, professional copywriting, photos, ongoing SEO, managed ads. If your salon is doing well, it can pay off.

The bad: expensive, slow (often 2–4 months from first call to launch), and overkill for a neighborhood barbershop. Agencies need to bill a lot of team hours to justify the price, and you feel it.

What about hosting and maintenance?

It's the most common trap. You get a low design price, and then hosting + maintenance costs $50–$150/month, "mandatory". Always ask upfront:

  • Is hosting included the first year?
  • How much does it cost from year 2?
  • Who pays for the domain, and who owns it?
  • Do I get access to my website's files and code?
  • Can I switch providers without losing my website?

If the answer to that last one is "no", be wary. Your website should be yours, not the provider's.

What should not cost extra

Some things are standard in 2026 and shouldn't inflate the price:

  • Responsive design (phone/tablet/desktop) — comes by default
  • SSL certificate (https://) — free with Let's Encrypt or Vercel
  • Basic Schema.org for SEO — written once and done
  • Open Graph for link previews in texts and social media — 5 minutes of work
  • Sitemap.xml and robots.txt — automatic in any modern setup

If these are billed as "extras", you're paying for nothing.

What about online booking? Does it cost extra?

It depends on what you use:

  • Call or text button: $0. The simplest option, and popular with neighborhood shops.
  • Booksy/Fresha/Vagaro integration: usually $0 to connect — you pay the platform's own subscription and fees. More in our Booksy vs Fresha vs Vagaro comparison.
  • Custom booking system built into your website: a bigger project, quoted separately. We built one for Nacho Huici in Pamplona, Spain: a calendar per barber, no third-party commissions.

Summary: which to choose

  • You just opened and have no budget: a DIY builder with a decent template. Upgrade when you can.
  • You've been open 1–3 years and want to look professional: a freelancer with a strong portfolio, or a pay-only-if-you-love-it studio.
  • Established salon doing well: a Pro website with an SEO blog, polished branding and built-in booking. New clients usually pay it back.
  • Chain, several locations or premium salon: an agency with beauty experience, plus monthly maintenance.
— Key point

Price is rarely the deciding factor. What matters is who builds it, exactly what's included, and whether you own it. A well-built affordable website beats a generic expensive one.

FAQ

Does a small salon need its own website, or is Booksy enough?

It depends on your volume and goals. If you just want bookings, Booksy may be enough. If you want to show up on Google by name, win new clients from local searches or have your own identity online, you need a website. We cover it in detail in this article.

How long does a salon website take?

A simple freelance site: 2–4 weeks. An agency site: 2–4 months. With a pay-only-if-you-love-it studio: a working preview in 24 hours, final launch in 1–3 weeks.

How much does monthly maintenance cost?

Standard hosting (Vercel, Netlify and similar) costs between $0 and about $20/month. If a provider charges $50–$150/month "mandatory" just to keep your site online, ask exactly what that includes.

Can I switch providers without losing my website?

Yes, as long as the provider hands over the source code or puts the platform account in your name. Get it in writing before you hire. If they say it's not possible, find another provider.

Is local SEO worth it for a salon?

Almost always. Salons in Google's "local pack" (the 3 map results) get most of the clicks in their area, making it one of the best-value investments in local marketing. We cover it in our local SEO guide.

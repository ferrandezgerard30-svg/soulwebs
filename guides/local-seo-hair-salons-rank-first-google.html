Local SEO for hair salons: how to rank first on Google

The complete guide to getting your salon into Google's top 3: Google Business Profile, reviews, local keywords and local links.

📅 November 5, 2026 ⏱ 11 min read By Soul Webs

For hair salons and barbershops, local SEO is one of the best-value things you can do for your marketing. The businesses in Google's top 3 "local pack" (the map results) get the large majority of clicks from people who are ready to book.

This guide covers the 6 factors Google uses to rank salons in local search, in order of real-world impact.

1. Google Business Profile (factor #1)

The Google Business Profile (formerly Google My Business) is the most important local SEO tool. It's what shows up on Google Maps with your name, address, hours and reviews.

How to set it up right

  1. Go to google.com/business and claim or create your profile.
  2. Business name: use exactly the name on your storefront. No "Best Cheap Hair Salon Downtown Austin" — that's keyword stuffing and Google can suspend you for it.
  3. Primary category: pick the most specific one. "Hair salon" or "Barber shop", not "Beauty services".
  4. Secondary categories: add 2–4 related ones, for example "Beauty salon" or "Hair extension technician".
  5. Full hours including holiday hours and any midday breaks.
  6. Photos: at least 10 photos of your space, team and work. Add 1–2 new photos every week.
  7. Description of up to 750 characters, with keywords used naturally.

2. Google reviews (factor #2)

Google reviews are the second most important factor in local SEO. In many searches, a salon with 80 reviews at 4.6 stars will outrank one with 20 reviews at 4.9 stars.

How to get honest reviews

  • Ask right away: just after the appointment, when the client is happy.
  • Make it easy: put a QR code on the mirror or the receipt that links straight to your review page.
  • Ask every client the same way. Don't offer anything in exchange for reviews and don't only ask happy clients — both break Google's rules.
  • Reply to every review, positive and negative. Google rewards active profiles.
"A polite, helpful reply to a negative review can do more for you than three extra positive ones. It shows the business is alive and cares."

3. Local keywords on your website

Your website should include the location keywords your customers search for. The most important ones for a salon are:

  • [type of service] + [city]: "hair salon in Austin", "barbershop in Miami"
  • [type of service] + [neighborhood]: "hair salon in Williamsburg", "barbershop in Wicker Park"
  • [specific service] + [city]: "skin fade Chicago", "balayage San Diego"

Where to put them:

  1. Title of the homepage: "Soul Hair · Hair Salon in Williamsburg, Brooklyn"
  2. Meta description of the homepage
  3. H1 heading
  4. About us text
  5. Image alt text for photos of the salon
  6. Footer text with your full address
— Careful

Don't repeat "hair salon Austin" 50 times on the page. Google penalizes keyword stuffing. The rule: keywords should appear naturally in sentences a real person would read.

4. Schema.org LocalBusiness

Schema.org is structured code that tells Google what kind of business you are with verifiable details: address, phone, hours, service area and services.

For a salon, the minimum useful schema looks like this:

{ "@context": "https://schema.org", "@type": "HairSalon", "name": "Soul Hair", "image": "https://soulhair.com/salon.jpg", "address": { "@type": "PostalAddress", "streetAddress": "123 Main St", "addressLocality": "Austin", "addressRegion": "TX", "postalCode": "78701", "addressCountry": "US" }, "telephone": "+15125550123", "openingHours": "Mo-Sa 09:00-20:00", "priceRange": "$$" }

Recommended business types for your salon:

  • HairSalon — hair salons
  • BarberShop — barbershops
  • BeautySalon — beauty salons with skin and nail services

5. Local links

A backlink is a link from another website to yours. Google treats them like "votes of confidence". For local SEO, the most valuable ones come from:

  • Local directories: Yelp, Apple Maps (Business Connect), Bing Places, the Better Business Bureau and industry directories like StyleSeat.
  • Local news: if a local paper or blog mentions you, ask for a link.
  • Other neighborhood businesses: partner with the coffee shop or florist next door and link to each other where it makes sense.
  • Business associations: your local chamber of commerce usually lists its members.
  • Nearby colleges: if you offer student discounts, they often have pages listing local partners.

Heads up: don't buy backlinks. Google detects them and penalizes you. Five real links beat 50 bought ones.

6. Speed and mobile

Most people search for salons on their phones. Google measures page experience with Core Web Vitals, and slow sites lose customers before they even load.

Target numbers

  • LCP (Largest Contentful Paint): under 2.5 seconds
  • CLS (Cumulative Layout Shift): under 0.1
  • INP (Interaction to Next Paint): under 200 ms

If your site runs on WordPress with lots of plugins, your scores are probably poor. Modern hosting like Vercel or Netlify is fast by default.

What not to do in 2026

  • Buying fake reviews. Google detects them and can remove reviews or suspend your profile. In the US, the FTC also fines businesses for fake reviews.
  • Creating several profiles for the same location. It's spam.
  • Keyword stuffing on your site ("hair salon Austin cheap hair salon Austin"). A guaranteed penalty.
  • Buying backlinks from link networks. A red flag for Google.
  • Copying text from another salon's website. Duplicate content hurts you both.

FAQ

How long does it take for a salon to rank first on Google?

In a low-competition area, a well set-up Google Business Profile with 15–20 real reviews can reach the local pack within weeks. In competitive areas (downtown in a big city), expect 3–6 months. Nobody can guarantee a #1 spot.

Is local SEO or Google Ads better for a salon?

Local SEO brings steady traffic without paying per click. Google Ads is faster, but you pay for every click. The best approach is often both: local SEO as the foundation, ads as a boost for launches or promotions.

Do I need a blog for local SEO?

It's not required, but it helps. A blog with 8–12 articles on cuts, hair care and trends helps you rank for long-tail keywords. That's part of our Pro Website plan.

Does being on Yelp help a salon?

Yes. In the US, Yelp is one of the most-used review sites for local services, and Apple Maps pulls ratings from it. Keep your Yelp listing complete and your name, address and phone identical to your Google profile.

How many reviews do I need to rank first?

It depends on local competition. If nearby salons have 100+ reviews, you'll need something similar. In less crowded areas, 30–50 real reviews with a good rating can put you in the top 3.
